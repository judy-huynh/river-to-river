The void

A figure ground, inverted. Everything privately held is solid and unlabelled. Everything you are allowed to stand in is cut out of it: the streets, the parks, the two rivers. Read this way 42nd Street is not a line on a plan, it is the longest continuous piece of public space between the Hudson and the East River, and it is only sixty feet wide.

A limit worth knowing. Below roughly zoom 13.4 the tile service stops sending minor streets, and a figure ground with no minor streets is just a black rectangle. So this view is pinned at the tightest framing that still holds the whole argument, and you pan to the rivers rather than zooming out to them. Why invert it. A normal plan draws the buildings as the subject and leaves the street as leftover white. Giambattista Nolli drew Rome the other way round in 1748 and made the public realm the figure. Doing that here makes the argument before a single number is added: this street is a thin, precious, almost accidental piece of public ground, and it is bounded by two rivers nobody thinks of as connected.
