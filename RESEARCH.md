# 42nd Street: what has already been proposed, and where the gaps are

Research pass, 15 Aug 2026. Feeds the centrepiece in `mockups/H-42nd-street.html`.
Everything below is sourced. The gap list at the bottom is the argument.

---

## What already exists

### 1. vision42 (1994 → 2015, still the anchor proposal)
A river to river, auto free light rail boulevard. Traces back to a 1994 City Council plan that was "approved overwhelmingly", revived by the Institute for Rational Urban Mobility, funded by the New York Community Trust from 2004 for technical studies on traffic, cost, phasing and economics. Ran an international design competition through The Architect's Newspaper with 123 entries, four finalists.

Their numbers, which everyone else still quotes:
- ~500,000 pedestrians a day on 42nd Street
- pedestrians outnumber motorists at least **5 to 1**
- the crosstown bus is often **slower than walking** (vision42's claim, not checked here. What the MTA's speed data shows is in `METHODOLOGY.md` section 3c)
- 85% of Midtown commuters arrive by transit
- Times Square alone draws ~165,000 people a day
- at grade light rail costs roughly **one tenth per mile** of subway
- 12 pairs of stops along the street, plus 2 pairs at the river ends

Source: [vision42.org](https://vision42.org/), [The Purpose](https://vision42.org/?page_id=38), [Technical Studies](https://vision42.org/?page_id=5941), [design competition](https://vision42.archpaper.com/), [Streetsblog, 2015](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2015/01/13/with-new-conceptual-designs-group-seeks-support-for-car-free-42nd-street)

### 2. KPF, "A Fix for 42nd Street"
Streetcar plus bike lanes, doubled pedestrian realm, the street as a linear plaza with seating, greenery and performance space. Their framing is the useful part: **the avenues got the interventions and 42nd Street itself was overlooked.**
Source: [KPF](https://www.kpf.com/story/a-fix-for-42nd-street)

### 3. The busway that has not happened
Manhattan community boards formally asked the city to turn 42nd Street into a busway. DOT said it would review it. Meanwhile the same agency advanced a 34th Street busway, extended double bus lanes on Madison from 42nd south to 23rd, and proposed 6th Avenue bus lanes for 51,000 daily riders.
Source: [Streetsblog, Jan 2025](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2025/01/10/midtown-to-adams-its-time-for-a-42nd-street-busway), [1010 WINS](https://www.audacy.com/1010wins/news/local/manhattan-community-boards-want-42nd-street-to-be-a-busway), [NYC DOT, 34th St](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2025/propose-busway-34-street-manhattan.shtml), [NYC DOT, Madison Ave](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/fast-buses-mamdani-moves-forward-madison-ave-bus-lane.shtml)

### 4. The $10bn thing actually being built
Port Authority Bus Terminal replacement, Foster + Partners with Epstein. Two blocks, 8th to 9th, 40th to 42nd. Groundbreaking done, completion targeted 2032. Five bus floors, 40 intercity gates, storage for 300 to 350 buses instead of 50, ramps direct to the Lincoln Tunnel, and a proposal to **permanently close 41st Street between 8th and 9th**. $1.9bn from USDOT, up to $2bn from property tax on new commercial towers.
Source: [PANYNJ](https://www.panynj.gov/port-authority/en/press-room/press-release-archives/2024-Press-Releases/port-authority-s-transformational-project-to-replace-midtown-bus.html), [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/be-careful-what-you-wish-for-port-authority-breaks-ground-on-new-midtown-bus-terminal), [6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/construction-begins-on-new-10b-port-authority-bus-terminal/), [NY YIMBY renderings](https://newyorkyimby.com/2024/06/new-renderings-reveal-port-authority-bus-terminal-overhaul-in-midtown-manhattan.html)

### 5. East Midtown and the Vanderbilt Corridor
73 blocks around Grand Central rezoned to unlock unused air rights. One Vanderbilt, at 42nd and Vanderbilt, bought its floor area with ~$210m of Grand Central infrastructure. Mandated sidewalk widenings on Madison and Lexington, Vanderbilt Avenue turned into a pedestrian gateway.
Source: [DCP Vanderbilt Corridor](https://alpha.nyc.gov/site/planning/plans/vanderbilt-corridor/vanderbilt-corridor.page), [DCP East Midtown](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/plans/east-midtown/east_midtown.pdf), [CityLand](https://www.citylandnyc.org/one-vanderbilt-construction-begins/), [amNY on air rights](https://www.amny.com/news/unlocking-unused-air-rights-key-midtown-east-rezoning-vision/)

### 6. Midtown South rezoning, which stops one block short
42 blocks, West 23rd to **West 40th**, Fifth to Eighth. Roughly 9,700 homes in an area where housing was previously not allowed, plus office to residential conversions helped by the 467-m abatement. On the east side, the old Pfizer headquarters on East 42nd is converting to 1,600+ units.
Source: [6sqft, CPC approval](https://www.6sqft.com/city-planning-commission-approves-midtown-south-rezoning/), [Ariel Property Advisors](https://arielpa.nyc/insights/250930-new-york-city-tackles-housing-crisis-with-sweeping-midtown-south-rezoning-plan), [NYREJ](https://nyrej.com/the-new-core-of-midtown-how-rezoning-is-transforming-30th-42nd-streets-by-joseph-aquino), [Manhattan Institute critique](https://manhattan.institute/article/thinking-again-about-midtown-south-nyc-new-plan-is-a-mass-of-contradictions)

### 7. Who actually lives there
Manhattan Plaza, just off 42nd in Hell's Kitchen: 1,689 units, about 70% performing artists, the rest elderly and community residents, Section 8 subsidised. Tudor City at the east end. Theatre Row and its six stages on West 42nd.
Source: [Related](https://www.related.com/our-company/properties/manhattan-plaza), [NYC LGBT Historic Sites](https://www.nyclgbtsites.org/site/manhattan-plaza/), [42nd Street, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_Street_(Manhattan))

### 8. The ground floor, as currently reported
Storefront vacancy in the "42BELOW" corridor down 19% in two years, occupancy growing 65% faster than Manhattan overall. Times Square itself still carrying elevated vacancy.
Source: [Live XYZ report](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/19/3259164/0/en/Live-XYZ-Unveils-Report-Showing-Midtown-South-Corridor-42BELOW-Retail-Occupancy-Growing-65-Faster-Than-Manhattan-Overall.html), [REBNY H1 2026](https://www.rebny.com/press-release/new-rebny-report-finds-manhattan-retail-momentum-extending-beyond-prime-corridors-in-first-half-of-2026/)

## The gaps. This is the untapped potential.

**1. Everybody plans the middle mile.**
Almost every proposal concentrates between 8th Avenue and Lexington: Times Square, Bryant Park, the library, Grand Central. vision42 gives the two river ends a single pair of stops each, at the very end of a 14 pair list. West of 9th and east of 2nd is effectively unplanned. Those ends are where the residents and both waterfronts are.

**2. Nobody counts the residents.**
Every vision is written for commuters, tourists and bus riders. The street has real addresses at both ends, Manhattan Plaza and Tudor City, and a working theatre district. 42nd is treated as a corridor, never as somewhere people live. The neighbour is the missing lens in the professional literature, which is exactly the lens a person who has actually walked it would bring.

**3. Every plan is a cross section, not a length.**
vision42 and KPF both propose one repeated street section for nearly two miles. But 42nd Street is a bus terminal, then a tourist plaza, then a park, then a library, then a train station, then an office canyon, then a housing estate, then the UN. A single section applied river to river is the error. A horizontal strip is the format that proves it, which is the whole reason the centrepiece works.

**4. The street wall is unmeasured.**
Every proposal is about the roadway. Retail reporting covers corridors and vacancy rates, not frontage. Nobody has published a foot by foot inventory of what the ground floor of 42nd Street actually does, which is the thing a pedestrian feels every three seconds.

**5. Unused floor area is only argued east of Fifth.**
The entire East Midtown rezoning is premised on unlocking unused air rights near Grand Central. Nobody has run that arithmetic west of Eighth. And the Midtown South rezoning deliberately stops at **West 40th**, one block short. There is a literal one block gap in the city's own plan and it is the block in question.

**6. None of the plans have been drawn on the same drawing.**
A $10bn bus terminal finishing in 2032, a busway that has been asked for but not designed, a light rail vision from 1994, a rezoning that stops at 40th, a pedestrian gateway at Vanderbilt, a 1,600 unit conversion on East 42nd. They overlap, they conflict, and no published drawing shows them all against one another along the length of the street.

**7. Time is missing.**
Every proposal is a static end state render. Nobody shows the same block at 7am, 1pm, 8pm and 2am, which is the actual difference between the blocks and the thing that decides whether a place feels safe.

**8. The famous numbers are old.**
The 500,000 pedestrians and the 5 to 1 ratio predate the pandemic, remote work, the Times Square plaza build out and congestion pricing. Nobody has publicly recounted 42nd Street since. Recounting it is a small, finishable, genuinely new contribution.

## What this means for the centrepiece

Gaps 1, 3 and 6 are the ones the strip is uniquely built to show. Recommended addition:

**A "who is already planning this" ribbon.** One band under the street showing the extent of every existing proposal: the Port Authority footprint to 2032, the Midtown South rezoning boundary stopping dead at 40th, the Vanderbilt corridor, the busway request, vision42's stop spacing. Drawn to scale, the blank stretches in that ribbon **are** the untapped potential. It turns the argument from an opinion into a picture.

Second addition, cheaper: **a time slider**, 7am / 1pm / 8pm / 2am, changing the ground floor ribbon. Answers gap 7 and makes the instrument feel alive without any new data collection beyond a few walks.
